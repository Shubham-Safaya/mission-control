Day 88. The role of boredom in data quality, an appreciation. Every identity incident I have investigated traces back to a moment where something boring was skipped. The unreviewed normalizer change. The vendor file loaded without a row-count sanity check. The threshold copied from the previous quarter without rerunning calibration. The merge alert nobody wired to a pager because merges had never spiked before. Meanwhile every genuinely resilient system I have seen is boring by design: checklists, gates, sampled reviews, dashboards checked on a schedule rather than after an incident. Nothing about it demos well. All of it compounds. The industry celebrates the algorithmic frontier, and the frontier matters. But the difference between identity platforms that enterprises trust and ones they quietly route around is almost never the matcher. It is operational tedium, faithfully executed, for years. Excitement is for prototypes. Production is a discipline of boredom, and the discipline is the product.
